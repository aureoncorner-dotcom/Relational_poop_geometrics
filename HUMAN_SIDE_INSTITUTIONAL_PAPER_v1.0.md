## Abstract

The recovered record supports a bounded conclusion: several independently documented routes connect private constituencies, personal financial interests, technology vendors, advocacy networks, and formal state authority. The strongest routes are public or recoverable rather than hidden. They include a White House–DOJ religious-policy structure; a dated sequence linking Todd Blanche's disclosed crypto interests, ethics obligations, a department-wide enforcement memorandum, and later reported transactions; an enumerated Palantir contract footprint and a narrow CMS/HHS → ICE → Palantir data-custody route; public media amplification and private access networks; and a durable judicial selection, confirmation, and advocacy pipeline. `[RP-01–RP-05; CR-01–CR-08; PL-01–PL-09; PA-01–PA-07; JU-01–JU-11. Sources: SRC-RP-001, SRC-RP-002, SRC-RP-003, SRC-RP-004, SRC-RP-005, SRC-CR-001, SRC-CR-002, SRC-CR-003, SRC-CR-004, SRC-PL-001, SRC-PL-002, SRC-PL-008, SRC-PL-009, SRC-PL-012, SRC-PA-008, SRC-PA-009, SRC-PA-010, SRC-PA-011, SRC-JU-001, SRC-JU-002, SRC-JU-003, SRC-JU-005, SRC-JU-006]`

The same record does **not** establish a unified command center. It does not show that the disputed Medicaid data was ingested into an enforcement platform or used against a person; that World Liberty's business relationship caused the Zhao pardon; that Blanche committed a §208 violation; that Barbaccia or Patel steered a Palantir matter; that Berger caused a Johnson decision; that Dialog coordinated an official action; that Seid's later $1.6B-scale transfer paid for the three named Trump Supreme Court confirmations; or that hospitality and property transactions purchased judicial outcomes. Those propositions remain held, not established, or retired for specific evidentiary reasons. `[CR-08, CR-09, TC-04, PL-07, PL-11, PL-13, PL-14, PA-04, PA-08, JU-05–JU-10. Sources/limits: SRC-LAW-001, SRC-LAW-002, SRC-CR-005, SRC-TC-006–SRC-TC-009, SRC-PL-009, SRC-PL-012, SRC-PL-013–SRC-PL-018, SRC-PA-002–SRC-PA-007, SRC-JU-007–SRC-JU-023]`

This distinction is the paper's central result. Institutional power often travels through ordinary-looking mechanisms—appointments, executive orders, guidance, contracting, data-sharing, media pressure, professional networks, nominations, scheduling, and disclosure rules. Demonstrating a route is analytically important. It is not, without the missing join, proof of unlawful capture, corrupt intent, or common command.

## I. Method: prove routes, then test consequences

### 1. Revision control

The controlling reconciliation governs later corrections. The canonical handoff supplies the prior claim-state and keeps its explicit nulls, counterevidence, retired arrows, and do-not-state boundaries alive. The raw investigation is a 48-page conversation export with stale internal citation tokens but no recoverable embedded source locators. It is useful for recovering names and acquisition targets; it is not authority over later corrections. The conceptual “Architecture of Capture” file has no external source apparatus and is used, at most, as method. Neither model repetition nor agreement counts as corroboration. `[Internal provenance audit: LOG-01]`

### 2. Component-level proof

A source can establish one component without upgrading a compound proposition. A financial disclosure can establish an asset bracket but not legal noncompliance. A contract can establish an award or ceiling but not use, efficacy, or personal steering. A registration list can establish planned participation but not attendance, agreement, or action. A court filing can establish what a party represented or argued, while a court order controls the findings and relief it actually entered. These distinctions are encoded claim by claim in the companion registry.

The paper therefore uses three independent dimensions:

- **Finding type:** fact, architecture, legal element, causal join, or intent/consideration.
- **Evidence state:** established, provisionally established, contested, open, not established, indeterminate, or retired/corrected.
- **Action:** advance, hold, close on the current record, or wrong instrument.

An “established architecture” may coexist with an “open causal join.” A “not established” proposition is not necessarily false; it means the reviewed record does not carry it. A “retired” arrow remains visible so that attractive but defeated claims are not silently reintroduced.

### 3. Independence and concentration

Sources are counted by evidence family, not headline count. Reuters and AP applied different classifications to one OGE filing; they are two analyses, not two independent measurements of the underlying income components. Several reports of the Medicaid dispute draw on one federal docket. Much of the 2026 Dialog reporting traces to one leaked dataset. Multiple justice-related rows depend heavily on one outlet's reporting and linked records. Those concentrations do not nullify the evidence, but they limit how far repetition can move a claim. `[Source-family audit: SRC-TC-001, SRC-TC-002, SRC-TC-003; SRC-PL-008–SRC-PL-012; SRC-PA-004, SRC-PA-005; SRC-JU-008–SRC-JU-014; LOG-01]`

### 4. Null results and missing records

A failed public search is not proof that an instrument does not exist. An unanswered request is not proof of no clearance. A party's “no responsive records” response, if later obtained, would establish only the result of that office's scoped search. Selected rows that yielded no support are called **null results**, not negative controls. The sample was non-random, and symbolic material influenced the historical selection of some actors; neither symbols nor selection supply evidentiary weight. Accordingly, this paper makes no prevalence or “unusualness” claim without a comparison class.

### 5. The join rule

Cross-packet coordination is stated only where a source demonstrates the actual bridge. Shared ideology, a common vendor, chronological adjacency, attendance, or a recurring name is not enough. The resulting paper is about separately proven institutional routes and narrow joins, not an asserted single system.

## II. Religious-policy routing: a public administrative route, an open capture question

### 1. The formal structure

Executive Order 14205 placed the White House Faith Office in the Domestic Policy Council and authorized it to consult outside faith leaders, recommend policy changes, coordinate with agencies, promote federal grant access, work with private actors, and collaborate with the Attorney General on religious-liberty enforcement concerns. The order also created an agency-liaison structure. Those are direct institutional facts, not inferences from personal proximity. `[RP-01, RP-02, RP-03. Source: SRC-RP-001]`

Executive Order 14202 separately created a DOJ-centered Task Force to Eradicate Anti-Christian Bias, chaired by the Attorney General and drawing members from a broad set of departments and offices. DOJ's official account of the inaugural meeting identifies senior officials from the Faith Office, DOJ, Defense, FBI, OMB, and other agencies. The evidence proves a public interagency route and official participation; it does not establish an off-record hierarchy or validate every participant's factual characterization. `[RP-04. Sources: SRC-RP-002, SRC-RP-003]`

Executive Order 14291 added a Religious Liberty Commission with an advisory relationship to the Faith Office and Domestic Policy Council and with DOJ support. But the order also supplied a temporal control: the Commission terminates on 4 July 2026 unless extended. No extension instrument was recovered in this pass, so the paper describes the Commission's creation and authorized route but holds any claim that it remained operational after that date. `[RP-05. Source: SRC-RP-004; missing instrument: extension order or other controlling continuation record]`

### 2. Administrative conversion

The route crossed from organization into administration. DOJ's July 2026 announcement says updated religious-liberty guidance reached federal programs, employment, contracting, rulemaking, and enforcement. The 2026 task-force report is evidence that the machinery produced a multi-agency output, although the report's assertions about prior administrations remain the task force's positions unless independently established. `[RP-05. Sources: SRC-RP-005, SRC-RP-006]`

This is the strongest defensible finding in the corridor: religious-policy interpretation had a formal route from the White House through DOJ and into named administrative domains. That route matters because it shows how an outside-facing constituency office can become policy guidance. Yet formal coordination around religious liberty is also a conventional executive function. The route alone does not show that any organization received an unlawful preference or that a competing constituency was excluded.

### 3. The missing consequence

`RP-06` therefore remains open. The record would have to identify a particular outside organization, a pre-decision request or recommendation, a resulting grant/contract/enforcement or rulemaking action, the governing exception, and a relevant comparator. Without that chain, “capture” would be a label placed on established administration rather than a finding about selective consequence. `[RP-06. No supporting external source; REQ-RP-01]`

## III. Financial interests and state policy

### A. Todd Blanche: a strong chronology and an unresolved legal pathway

#### 1. Entry interests and ethics obligations

Blanche's public disclosure identifies crypto and crypto-related interests in filing-defined brackets. His signed ethics agreement imposed divestiture and interim-recusal commitments for covered matters affecting specified interests. The filings establish what was disclosed and promised; they do not establish that a later act fell within §208 or breached the agreement. `[CR-01, CR-02. Sources: SRC-CR-001, SRC-CR-002, SRC-LAW-001]`

#### 2. Policy action before reported transactions

On 7 April 2025, Blanche issued “Ending Regulation By Prosecution.” The memorandum disbanded the National Cryptocurrency Enforcement Team and narrowed specified platform- and registration-focused enforcement routes. It also retained priorities involving fraud, theft, terrorism, narcotics, trafficking, organized crime, hacking, and victimization. The policy change must be described in both directions; “DOJ ended crypto enforcement” would be false compression. `[CR-03, CR-04. Source: SRC-CR-003]`

The later periodic transaction report records late-May and 2 June transactions involving relevant assets, placing the memorandum before the reported mitigation. The same report contains a reviewing note that some assets were gifted “in their entirety” to adult children or a grandchild while transaction rows also list sales involving some of those asset categories. This internal filing ambiguity prevents the paper from saying either “all sold” or “all gifted.” It establishes a sequence and a records question, not a completed legal analysis. `[CR-03, CR-05, U-08. Source: SRC-CR-004]`

#### 3. What “cleared” does and does not prove

ProPublica reported DOJ's position that Blanche's participation was flagged, addressed, and cleared in advance. That is affirmative counterevidence and must remain adjacent to the concern. It is established as an attributed DOJ position, but the recovered statement does not identify the decisionmaker, the legal route, the instrument, the scope, or the reasoning. `[CR-06. Source: SRC-CR-005]`

Senators later requested the underlying resolution and related communications, and Senate Judiciary Democrats described some requests as unanswered or insufficiently answered. Those records establish oversight activity and the committee's stated response status. They do not establish that no clearance exists. The Campaign Legal Center complaint is an interested-party legal allegation and acquisition lead, not an OIG finding. `[CR-07. Sources: SRC-CR-006, SRC-CR-007, SRC-CR-008]`

#### 4. The correct §208 question

The unresolved legal issue is not “holdings plus policy equals violation.” It is whether the 7 April act was a covered particular matter—potentially one of general applicability—with a direct and predictable effect on Blanche's financial interests, and, if so, whether participation was authorized by an individual waiver, a regulatory exemption, a no-covered-matter/no-direct-effect analysis, or an effective recusal/screening mechanism. A Part 2640 exemption or a conclusion that no individualized instrument was required would make an empty waiver search nondispositive. `[CR-08. Sources: SRC-LAW-001, SRC-LAW-002, SRC-CR-002, SRC-CR-003, SRC-CR-005; missing: REQ-CR-01]`

No recovered source proves bribery, a coordinated trade, direct consideration, or intentional self-enrichment. That row is closed on the current record, subject to reopening on direct evidence. It is not an exoneration and cannot be revived by the chronology alone. `[CR-09. Bounded null result: LOG-01; reviewed sources SRC-CR-001–SRC-CR-008]`

## IV. Palantir: contract footprint, dependency, custody, and person-level limits

### 1. Enumerated federal footprint

The public instruments support a multi-environment footprint, but only at enumerated scope. A Defense contract notice records a $795M modification, P00005 to W911QX-24-D-0012, for Maven Smart System licenses. An Army announcement describes an enterprise agreement consolidating 75 contracts for up to ten years with a $10B maximum potential. The Army expressly says that ceiling is not a specific obligation or commitment. An ICE award record supplies a separate immigration-enforcement environment. `[PL-01, PL-02, PL-03. Sources: SRC-PL-001, SRC-PL-002, SRC-PL-003]`

These instruments establish contracts and ceilings, not actual final spending, efficacy, correctness, personal sponsorship, or a common agency. Overlapping Maven amounts cannot be aggregated without the order chain. A vendor appearing in multiple agencies is a shared technological substrate; it is not evidence that the agencies or officials share a command structure.

### 2. ICE dependency and the procurement conflict

ICE's 2025 limited-sources justification and a 2026 sole-source notice support a real incumbency/dependency question. But the procurement record is not internally clean. A 2022 SAM record labels the authority “Only One Source,” while FPDS-derived fields have been reported as full/open, multiple-award fair opportunity with one offer; a later justification uses logical-follow-on language. The original award cannot responsibly be called “competitively awarded” or “sole-sourced” until the complete 2022 justification and award history are reconciled. `[PL-04. Sources: SRC-PL-003, SRC-PL-004, SRC-PL-005, SRC-PL-020, SRC-PL-021; missing: REQ-PL-01]`

Even a fully established limited-source route would not itself prove improper lock-in. Migration cost, interoperability, intellectual property, urgency, and continuity can lawfully support constrained sourcing. A claim that the dependency was unusual or avoidable needs market research, alternatives, a comparison class, and the full J&A—not simply a recurring vendor name.

### 3. Formal data-sharing rules

CMS policy describes an Information Exchange Agreement mechanism for protected interagency data exchange. That establishes a general process requirement, not that a particular IEA existed, that this exact transfer required one under a specified legal theory, or that a specific instrument was breached. The operative agreement and its scope remain an acquisition target. `[PL-05. Source: SRC-PL-006]`

### 4. The CMS/HHS → ICE → Palantir custody route

The strongest Palantir finding is narrow and concrete. The preliminary-injunction order and later filings establish litigation over HHS/CMS data supplied to ICE. California's motion quoted government representations that ICE had a “large and complex” dataset, that it had not been ingested into officer-facing systems or used for law enforcement as of stated dates, and that it was quarantined while filtering was addressed. Those quotations are party representations carried in a motion, not final findings on every later event. `[PL-06, PL-08. Sources: SRC-PL-007, SRC-PL-008, SRC-PL-009]`

NPR/KCLU's July 2026 report, based on later docket materials, describes a Teams transfer of the dataset to Palantir personnel, an ICE request that Palantir delete it, Palantir's statement that it was purged, ICE's statement that it was not used for law-enforcement purposes, and later-discovered copies held by ICE users. The underlying declaration and exhibit have durable docket locators but still require direct attachment review before exact residual-copy counts carry publication weight. Taken together, the record supports the narrow custody route: CMS/HHS data → ICE → Palantir personnel. `[PL-06. Sources: SRC-PL-010 and SRC-PL-011 located but not fully ingested; source-bearing report SRC-PL-012]`

The route is important because it moves the issue from a hypothetical capability to a documented custody event. It also exposes the danger of overstatement. A Teams transfer is not proof of ingestion into ELITE or another operational platform. No recovered source links this disputed dataset to a query, target, address, arrest, detention, or removal. The custody finding therefore advances; the downstream-use claim remains not established. `[PL-07. Sources/limits: SRC-PL-009, SRC-PL-012; missing: REQ-PL-02]`

The custody-control characterization is provisionally established and legally contested. Transfer, deletion requests, copy persistence, and court supervision support a governance concern. The no-ingestion/no-use representations and Palantir's purge statement are material counterevidence. Neither side may be dropped. `[PL-08. Sources: SRC-PL-008, SRC-PL-009, SRC-PL-012]`

### 5. Revolving doors and personal participation

GSA's primary announcement places Greg Barbaccia in the TTS role effective 19 February 2026; the 7 May date on a later page was an update, not the appointment. Nextgov later reported an end-of-August departure and a future plan to return to Palantir. As of the 17 August cutoff, the reported return had not occurred, and the precise role and start date were unclear. The paper therefore establishes a revolving-door sequence through a future plan, not completed re-employment. `[PL-09. Sources: SRC-PL-013, SRC-PL-014, SRC-PL-015]`

A secondary disclosure compilation reports an entry PLTR bracket for Barbaccia but no disposition. The appointment statute and OGE guidance indicate that a formal PAS-nominee ethics agreement may be the wrong instrument. The correct targets are agency-held OMB/GSA 278e and transaction reports, recusal and screening records, §208 advice or waivers if any, post-employment advice, and Palantir-specific participation. Stock retention and steering are not established. `[PL-10, PL-11. Sources: SRC-PL-016, SRC-LAW-004, SRC-LAW-005; missing: REQ-PL-03; bounded null: LOG-01]`

Kash Patel's signed ethics agreement required PLTR divestiture within 90 days and interim recusal; his transaction report records a 12 May 2025 sale in the $50,001–$100,000 bracket. That is established mitigation. It neither proves nor disproves matter-specific participation, and no recovered record connects Patel to a Palantir approval or procurement decision. `[PL-12, PL-13. Sources: SRC-PL-017, SRC-PL-018; missing: REQ-PL-04]`

A secondary compilation concerning Clark Minor identifies a possible HHS/Palantir chronology, but the controlling revision set has not adjudicated it and no record shows that Minor approved CMS-to-ICE or ICE-to-Palantir sharing. It remains a preserved `U-06` lead, not a new integrated arrow. `[U-06. Source lead: SRC-PL-019; missing: REQ-U-06]`

## V. Access and amplification: public pressure, residential proximity, and a private network

### 1. Bannon as a public amplifier

The cleanest consequential environment is the 2025 state-AI-law moratorium fight. Congress records the amendment removing the moratorium and the Senate's 99–1 vote. The Verge reports Bannon's public pressure in that fight, while AP documents a broad coalition of opponents. These sources support a public-amplification finding; the primary records prove the legislative outcome, not which advocate caused it. `[PA-01. Sources: SRC-PA-008, SRC-PA-009, SRC-PA-010, SRC-PA-011]`

A separate Washington Post report says Bannon used *War Room* to urge members to support Johnson during the 2025 Speaker contest. That second instance reinforces the existence of a public political apparatus, but alternative mechanisms included Trump's calls and commitments made by Johnson. Neither event supports a hidden backchannel or command of administration policy. `[PA-01, PA-02. Source: SRC-PA-001; bounded null: LOG-01]`

### 2. Johnson and Berger: access without a consequence join

ProPublica reported that Johnson and evangelical pastor Steve Berger shared a donor-owned Capitol Hill residence during a period when Berger was engaged in outside advocacy. The same source preserved Johnson's spokesperson's position that Johnson paid fair-market rent and did not discuss legislation or policy with Berger. The record establishes a bounded access setting, not a below-market gift or an influence result. `[PA-03. Source: SRC-PA-002]`

Semafor later reported that Johnson relocated roughly a month after the story and rented a different property. That bounds the earlier proximity period and prevents present-tense narration. The move does not establish motive or concede that influence occurred. `[PA-05. Source: SRC-PA-003]`

The missing arrow is Berger request/advice → Johnson contact or scheduling → identifiable action → consequence. No such chain was recovered. `PA-04` remains open and on HOLD. `[PA-04. Source lead/limits: SRC-PA-002; missing: REQ-PA-02]`

### 3. Dialog: a real access network and a failed event join

WIRED reported that it verified a leaked 2026 Dialog registration dataset and corrected a mistaken conflation involving two people named Jeff Epstein. The correction is material; it is not a wholesale retraction of the investigation. Axios independently supports Dialog's existence and founding but does not independently authenticate the 2026 roster. Palantir's issuer page establishes Peter Thiel's Palantir role. Together, these sources establish a private curated network and a narrow common-founder overlap. `[PA-06. Sources: SRC-PA-004, SRC-PA-005, SRC-PA-006]`

That overlap cannot carry a procurement or command claim. An invitation, registration, off-record norm, or session title is not evidence that members agreed, instructed an official, or produced a government action. Reporting derived from the same leaked dataset counts as one family even when republished by several outlets.

The planned Powerscourt event was cancelled before its 12–16 August dates. Accordingly, the claim that listed people were actually together at the 2026 retreat is retired. No reliable relocation or substitute event record was recovered. Registration is not attendance, and cancellation defeats the proposed event-based join. `[PA-07. Sources: SRC-PA-004, SRC-PA-007]`

No communication/agenda → common decision → attributable official action chain has been established for Dialog. `PA-08` is closed on the current record, with reopening conditioned on that kind of source. `[PA-08. Bounded null: LOG-01; missing: REQ-PA-03]`

## VI. Judiciary: production architecture, later funding, and justice-specific boundaries

### 1. Selection, scheduling, and advocacy

The Federalist Society's own biography says Leonard Leo advised Trump on judicial selection and assisted with the Gorsuch and Kavanaugh selection and confirmation processes; source-bearing reporting describes a wider network around that work. These sources establish a selection and advocacy role, not ownership of nominees or control of sitting justices. `[JU-01. Sources: SRC-JU-001, SRC-JU-009]`

Senate records show the institutional sequence: the Judiciary Committee took no action on Merrick Garland, the Senate later changed the cloture threshold, and Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett proceeded to recorded confirmations. McConnell's contemporaneous statement supplies the announced gatekeeping position. The evidence establishes decisive scheduling and procedural power exercised through constitutional institutions. It does not make that power unlawful or prove a private actor commanded it. `[JU-02. Sources: SRC-JU-002, SRC-JU-003, SRC-JU-004]`

The Judicial Crisis Network publicly announced multimillion-dollar campaigns around the vacancy and later confirmations, including a planned $10M effort and a $2.2M initial 2020 phase. JCN is an interested self-report, so the record supports announced advocacy at that scale while holding exact expenditure totals pending filings, invoices, and ad-buy records. Advocacy aimed at a confirmation is not evidence that the confirmed justice later obeyed the advocate. `[JU-03. Sources: SRC-JU-005, SRC-JU-006; missing: REQ-JU-01]`

### 2. Seid, Marble, and the chronology that kills an attractive arrow

Marble Freedom Trust's IRS-derived filing reports $1,603,791,362 in contributions for the relevant filing period, and ProPublica reconstructs the Barre Seid/Tripp Lite transfer and 2021 sale route. That establishes a later, enormous funding architecture associated with Leo. `[JU-04. Sources: SRC-JU-007, SRC-JU-008]`

It does **not** establish that Seid's $1.6B-scale transfer funded the Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, or Barrett confirmation campaigns. Gorsuch and Kavanaugh predate the Marble transfer structure at issue; Barrett was confirmed in October 2020, before the 2021 sale. The direct arrow is retired unless a separate, date-specific earlier transfer is produced. This correction strengthens the architecture by keeping later funding from flowing backward through time. `[JU-05. Sources: SRC-JU-002, SRC-JU-007, SRC-JU-008]`

### 3. Thomas and Crow

The recovered reporting and disclosure materials support a benefits/access and disclosure-history finding involving Clarence Thomas and Harlan Crow. They also preserve Thomas's “personal hospitality/family trips” position and later disclosure amendments. The applicable statute and the Court's Code supply legal standards, not a case-specific adjudication. `[JU-06. Sources: SRC-JU-010, SRC-JU-011, SRC-JU-012, SRC-JU-018, SRC-LAW-006, SRC-JU-016]`

No recovered record completes a Crow interest → specific case → Thomas knowledge/participation → consideration or outcome chain. The paper therefore advances the disclosure/access facts while holding any purchased-outcome claim.

### 4. Alito and Singer

ProPublica reports a 2008 Alaska trip involving Samuel Alito and Paul Singer and later Singer-related business before the Court. The NML Capital opinion provides primary counterevidence to a bespoke-vote theory: the disposition was 7–1. Alito's contrary disclosure/recusal position must remain in the record. The intersection supports an appearance and recusal question; it does not establish that Singer purchased Alito's vote or that the outcome was uniquely delivered for him. `[JU-07. Sources: SRC-JU-013, SRC-JU-019, SRC-LAW-006, SRC-JU-016]`

### 5. Gorsuch and Duffy

The recovered filing and reporting support a property transaction involving an entity in which Neil Gorsuch held an interest and a buyer associated with Greenberg Traurig, followed by later firm business before the Court. The filing's buyer transparency and the governing ethics framework are legitimate questions. Duffy's statement that he had not met or spoken with Gorsuch and consulted firm ethics personnel is affirmative counterevidence. Crude later win/loss ratios are not probative and are not used. No favorable-treatment join is established. `[JU-08. Sources: SRC-JU-014, SRC-JU-020, SRC-JU-021, SRC-LAW-006, SRC-JU-016]`

### 6. Roberts spouse recruiting

Reporting and a sworn allegation raise questions about Jane Roberts's recruiting compensation. Judicial Conference Advisory Opinion 107 is an important counterweight: a spouse's business relationships do not automatically require disqualification from every matter involving a contact. The factual chain needed here is commission → identified client or firm → identified case → Chief Justice knowledge and participation → case-specific recusal analysis. It has not been recovered, so `JU-09` remains open rather than advanced. `[JU-09. Sources: SRC-JU-017, SRC-JU-022, SRC-JU-023; missing: REQ-JU-03]`

### 7. The selected-sample null

The reviewed source set did not produce a comparable personal-benefactor or financial-capture chain for Kavanaugh or Barrett. Ordinary disclosed teaching, book, or other lawful income is not itself evidence of capture. This is a bounded null result in a non-random sample—not a negative control and not proof of universal absence. `[JU-10. Bounded null: LOG-01; acquisition portal SRC-JU-015]`

## Conclusion

The source-repaired record supports an institutional paper, but not the one the older rhetoric sometimes implied. It supports several distinct routes by which private organization can encounter formal power: constituency offices become guidance; personal financial interests coexist with policy authority; vendors become embedded and receive sensitive data; media and private networks create access and pressure; and selection, scheduling, advocacy, and tenure form a judicial production line.

Those are substantial findings because they identify mechanisms rather than moods. They show where accountability instruments belong: disclosure and ethics analysis, procurement records, data-governance agreements and logs, contact and scheduling records, financial transfers, and case-specific recusal evidence.

They do not justify a single-cabal explanation. The project is strongest where it says exactly which route exists, exactly which consequence does not yet follow, and exactly which instrument would decide the open element. On the present record, the defensible conclusion is **multiple documented corridors, several narrow joins, and no established common command**.
