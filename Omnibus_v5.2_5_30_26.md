2. Failure Modes

Bypass

O → S

Direct control without mediation.


------

Dissolution

Nothing is allowed to become load-bearing.

The system preserves exit, rotation, forkability, and replaceability so aggressively that no shared role, promise, protocol, or structure accumulates enough continuity to hold pressure, carry memory, or complete work.

Capture is R becoming required.
Dissolution is R never being permitted to matter.

Typical signs:

- every commitment remains provisional
- roles rotate before context can mature
- structures are forked before they are tested
- “keep it loose” becomes avoidance of decision
- exit remains valid, but commitment becomes suspect
- no shared invariant is protected long enough to build trust
- the room avoids capture but also avoids doing anything
- under pressure, the system scatters instead of flexing

Repair:

- name the minimum shared invariant that must hold for now
- protect it without making it sacred
- distinguish exitable from uncommitted
- let some roles carry continuity while remaining recallable
- review load-bearing commitments on a schedule instead of dissolving them reflexively

Stamp:

DISSOLUTION IS NOT FREEDOM.
IT IS THE FAILURE TO LET ANYTHING HOLD.


Pinch

O → R → S
      ↑
   bottleneck

R becomes too narrow to carry the interaction cleanly.

The mediator becomes gatekeeper, owner, authority, or necessary bridge.


---

10. Minimal Tests

Swap R

Change the mediator.

If the system collapses rather than adapts, capture is likely.


---

Overload R

Increase volume, narrow access, or force too much exchange through one mediator.

If distortion, delay, or dependency rises, pinch is likely.


---

Asymmetrize R

Make feedback harder to return than instruction.

If one side’s report becomes less legible, distorted return or capture may be developing.


---

Thin R

Reduce translation below the level needed for coherent return.

If brittleness spikes, bypass pressure is likely.


---

Add T

Wait and return.

Did time clarify, distort, fatigue, integrate, preserve, or decay the system?


---

Update O

Allow feedback to change the initiating intent.

If O cannot update, rigidity is likely.


---

Push Bypass

Reduce mediation and attempt direct O → S control.

Does the system recover, resist, or become brittle?

Tests exist to break the model, not defend it.
