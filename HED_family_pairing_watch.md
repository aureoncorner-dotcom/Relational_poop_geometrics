# HED-Family Pairing Watch Item

**Watch Item: HED-Family Pairing within Q1 2026 Fireball Activity**

**Status:** Open watch item  
**Scope:** Composition-only, tracked separately from directional/fireball-rate baseline

---

## 1. Observed Facts

- Q1 2026 shows elevated upper-tail fireball activity, not a clear increase in total counts.
- \~2,300 total events
- 40 events with 50+ reports (approximately 2× typical)
- 16 events with 100+ reports (approximately 2× typical)
- 33/40 large events associated with sonic booms

Two distinct radiant concentrations observed:
- Anthelion zone
- High-declination zone

Two meteorite-producing events within 9 days:
- March 8 (Western Europe) → suspected diogenite
- March 17 (Ohio) → confirmed eucrite

Angular separation between events: \~98.2 degrees → not from a common stream

**Historical baseline** (2015–2025 Meteoritical Bulletin):
- 116 classified falls
- 7 HED-classified falls (\~6.0%)

## 2. Baseline Explanation (Directional)

The working baseline for Q1 2026 is:  
**Directional geometry (Anthelion + high-declination) increases the rate of slow, recoverable events.**

This explains:
- why more large fireballs are observed
- why more meteorites are recoverable

It does **not** explain compositional clustering (e.g. HED pairing).

## 3. Anomaly Under Watch (Compositional)

**Observed anomaly:** Two HED-family meteorite falls within 9 days.

**Scope:**
- Not a shared-stream claim
- Not a global explanation
- Purely a composition coincidence under scrutiny

## 4. Probability Framing

**A. Conditional** (given two classified falls)  
Probability both are HED:  
p² ≈ (0.06)² ≈ 0.0036 → \~0.36% (1 in 275)  
*(Measures composition rarity only)*

**B. Post-hoc** (over full 2015–2025 record)  
Probability at least one 9-day HED-HED pairing appears somewhere: \~9%  
(\~4–5% under stricter assumptions)  
*(Measures whether such clustering can appear by chance)*

## 5. Interpretation

- The pair is unusual.
- But not yet statistically decisive.

**Current classification:** Watch item, not established clustering.

## 6. Assumptions

- Baseline uses 2015–2025 Meteoritical Bulletin valid falls
- Combines confirmed + probable falls
- Assumes constant HED rate (\~6%)
- Treats March 8 event as HED-family (pending confirmation)
- Uses uniform-in-time null model

## 7. Known Limitations

- Small sample size (7 historical HED falls)
- One event classification not yet confirmed
- Null model does not include source-specific composition variation, recovery bias, or directional filtering
- Radiant uncertainty: 10–20 degrees typical error
- Possible reporting effects: witness amplification not fully quantified

## 8. Strengthening Conditions

The anomaly becomes stronger if:
- March 8 fall is confirmed as HED
- Additional near-term HED falls occur
- HED fraction rises above baseline (\~6%)

## 9. Weakening / Falsification

The anomaly weakens if:
- March 8 event is not HED
- HED fraction remains near baseline
- No further clustering appears

**This is a cumulative test, not a single-event test.**

## 10. Tracking Protocol

Use future classified falls as denominator.

**Detection thresholds:**
- 4+ HED in next 20 falls → early signal
- 6+ HED in next 43 falls → strong signal (\~tripling)
- 14+ HED in next 140 falls → moderate signal (\~doubling)

**Negative indicators:**
- ≤3 HED in next 40 falls → weakens strong clustering
- ≤2 HED in next 60 falls → weakens moderate clustering

## 11. Current Read

Q1 2026 appears to be an upper-tail fireball anomaly driven largely by directional geometry (Anthelion + high-declination).  

The HED pairing is a separate compositional anomaly that is unusual but not yet statistically decisive.

## 12. Operational Summary

- Treat as watch item
- Do not merge with directional explanations, comet activity, or unrelated compositional anomalies
- Update only when new classified falls are added or classifications are confirmed/revised
