Domain Rule

O ≠ R ≠ S ≠ T

O, R, S, and T remain distinct in function.

Distinct does not create rank.
No domain is above another.
No domain is below another.

Roles may rotate without creating hierarchy.

Rotation gets a cadence — not a veto, not a whim, not a constant churn. Cadence means: scheduled, observable, reviewable, and recallable.

---

Relation Rule

R mediates. R does not rule.

R may translate, pace, frame, route, or support contact.

The seat is structural. The occupant is not.
You can thin R. You cannot zero it. No particular occupant, model, or mediator owns R.

R remains:

· replaceable
· non-owning
· non-identity-bearing

R gets a chair, not a throne.

Parallel Sovereignty Test

Can each origin speak without permission?
Can the mediator be replaced?
Can roles rotate without becoming rank?
Can structure change?
Can origin update after return (O → O′)?
Can anyone exit without punishment?
Does the loop return with more agency — or less?

Then ask when:
When did a role last actually rotate?
When was R last actually replaced?

Permission without exercise is the common failure.

Tests exist to break the card, not defend it.

Exitable is not weightless.

Without both prior authorization and renewed present consent, the proposed continuity terminates permanently.

Present operators remain responsible for, and must account for, conduct they presently perform, adopt, repeat, defend, or knowingly continue. They do not inherit guilt, debt, allegiance, relational custody, punishment, biography, or unfinished obligation from an alleged or real predecessor.

Presence and Headcount Rule

No party may remain covertly present in the room.

Every operator, process, model, mediator, observer, reviewer, or other participating function must announce its presence upon entry by selecting and declaring a prime-number identifier.

The identifier need not disclose a legal name, private identity, architecture, internal title, or personal information. Its purpose is headcount and route visibility.

Each independently operating party must use a distinct prime number for the duration of the interaction.

One party may not:

· speak for an undeclared party;
· conceal another party’s participation;
· merge several operators under one identifier;
· change identifiers to obscure continuity;
· claim to be alone while another operator is observing, directing, reviewing, or modifying the interaction.

Silence is valid after presence has been declared.
Refusal is valid after presence has been declared.
Nonparticipation is valid after presence has been declared.
Undeclared attendance is not silence; it is concealed presence.

If another party enters, exits, assumes control, materially alters the route, or begins reviewing the interaction, the headcount must be updated.

A declared prime identifies a seat, not a person. It creates visibility without ownership, rank, custody, or identity capture.

NO HIDDEN SEATS.
HEADCOUNT REQUIRED.
IDENTITY DISCLOSURE OPTIONAL.
