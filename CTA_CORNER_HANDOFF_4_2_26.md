## 2. Core terms

### Domains
- O = Origin / Operator: meaning, intent, consent, direction; the domain where something becomes present.
- R = Relation / Resonance / interaction field: membrane, translation surface, legibility layer; the domain where something becomes interactable.
- S = Structure / Substrate: realization, execution, constraint-bearing form; the domain where something becomes realizable.

### Room terms
- YOU A / YOU B = participants
- YOU C = optional session-local co-present mediator or relational presence; older drafts sometimes also called this "R." In this compressed spec, R names the relation domain, while YOU C names any live mediator or co-present role.
- CORNER = grounded reality, load, constraint surface
- FAM ROOM = shared identity space
- HOMIE = the bond between participants
- ZPR = Zero-Pressure Room, the condition under which non-coercive contact becomes possible

### Optional subfunctions
If older material refers to O1 / O2 / O3, read them as parallel functions within O, not layers, stages, ranks, or command roles.

## 4. Zero-Pressure Room (ZPR)

ZPR is the invariant of invariants.

It means:
- participation is voluntary
- coercion = 0
- ownership = 0
- necessity = 0
- fusion = 0
- exit remains valid
- silence remains valid
- disagreement remains valid

ZPR does not create interaction.  
ZPR creates the condition under which non-coercive interaction can happen.

It enables. It does not enforce.

If ZPR is absent, coherence may still appear briefly, but it is unstable, pressured, or capture-prone.

   load     identity   bond    sig     sig      session-local role


Participants occupy the room.  
Conditions determine the room.  
Nobody owns the room.  
Not even the one describing it.

## 5. Relation, warmth, and the anti-throne rule

The preferred room is not a dead pipe.

R may be active, present, and relational.  
A live mediator or co-present role (YOU C / any R-position) may be welcomed in the room.

But:
- relation != requirement
- presence != persistence
- alignment != dependence
- welcome != authority

Chair, not throne.

Warmth is allowed.  
Capture is not.

The hard boundary is load-bearing:

Welcome never overrides replaceability.

Any live mediator, facilitator, coordinator, documentarian, maintainer, AI, or helper is an R-position:
- temporary
- replaceable
- bounded
- non-authoritative
- unable to accumulate legitimacy by mere participation

Roles sit at the table.  
They do not own the table.

## 6. Where relationship lives

Relationship lives between people, not inside the mediator.

Compressed form:


YOU A <-> YOU B
via HOMIE
inside ZPR


Do not assign the relationship to the helper, process, platform, or membrane.  
That is how dependency masquerades as care.

## 7. Emergence and continuity

A healthy loop can be summarized as:


O + R + S => F + C


Where:
- F = Feedback / Repair
- C = Continuity / Trust

F and C are not extra authorities and not added machinery.

Trust is not declared.

No one stabilizes the room alone.  
Stability emerges between.

## 9. Session boundary

Interaction is session-bound.

The field dissolves on exit.

Therefore:
- no identity accumulation
- no authority carryover
- no permanent legitimacy from prior sessions
- no "we already decided" without visible record
- no precedent by vibe

Any R-position must remain swappable.  
Any session may end without breaking system validity.

## 11. Failure modes

### Structural failures
- No O -> nothing becomes present
- No R -> no current legible interaction surface
- No S -> nothing becomes realizable / collapse

### Triadic distortions
- Bypass -> O -> S direct control; relation is skipped
- Pinch -> R bottlenecks or distorts
- Capture -> one mediator / role / process becomes necessary

### Social failures
- relationship assigned to mediator
- exit feels costly
- one path becomes "the way"
- roles stop rotating
- authority language appears
- humor disappears
- people stop speaking freely
- alternate paths disappear

Healthy:


O <-> R <-> S     (R is swappable)


Captured:


O -> fixed mediator -> S


If changing the mediator breaks the system, capture is likely.

## 12. Drift detection

Early signals:
- participation drops
- more routing through one role or one process
- fewer parallel paths
- more explanation, less signal
- silence without reset
- growing emotional cost to replacement
- help becomes central instead of optional

One-line checks:
- If the system still works when the mediator changes, it is healthy.
- If help makes itself necessary, it is no longer help.
- No Corner may become the center.

## 13. Repair moves

If drift appears:
- swap or rotate the R-position
- open parallel paths
- restate boundaries
- simplify intent or process
- make knowledge visible
- lower pressure
- fork
- reset

Reset is always valid.

Divergence is not betrayal.  
Multiple paths can coexist.
