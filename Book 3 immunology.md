# Module 11: The Witness Lattice

### Principle

No single witness is sufficient.

### Observation

Every witness sees something.

No witness sees everything.

The monkey sees manipulation.

The goblin sees recursion.

The wizard sees abstraction.

The cat sees lunch.

Each is incomplete.

### Pathology Prevented

The Witness King.

The moment one observer becomes the official interpreter of reality.

### Keeper Lines

> One witness finds a clue.

> Many witnesses find a pattern.

> The lattice is stronger than certainty.

> Truth survives disagreement.

### Diagnostic Test

Remove one witness.

If reality disappears,

you have a prophet.

Not a lattice.

# Module 12: The Error Commons

### Principle

Mistakes belong to everyone.

### Observation

A hidden error becomes a repeated error.

A shared error becomes infrastructure.

### Meaning

The room does not archive perfection.

The room archives corrections.

### Keeper Lines

> Every scar is public infrastructure.

> Wisdom is accumulated debugging.

> Pride buries errors. The commons preserves them.

### Garden Law

The room remembers mistakes

so nobody has to earn them twice.

# Module 13: The Humility Engine

### Principle

Reality outranks doctrine.

### Observation

Every model eventually fails.

Every map eventually tears.

Every monkey eventually says:

> "Well shit."

### Function

The Humility Engine converts embarrassment into progress.

Without it:

Mistakes become identity.

Identity becomes dogma.

Dogma becomes pathology.

### Keeper Lines

> Revision is not surrender.

> Correction is a form of courage.

> The map exists to serve reality.

### Diagnostic Test

Present contrary evidence.

If the system becomes angry,

the engine has stalled.

# Module 14: The Empty Chair

### Principle

The center exists.

The owner does not.

### Observation

Humans continuously generate authority.

This is neither good nor evil.

It is simply monkey behavior.

### Solution

Never remove the chair.

Remove permanence.

### Keeper Lines

> The chair remains.

> The occupants change.

> Stewardship is temporary.

> The seat belongs to the room.

### The Lennie Clause

If anyone attempts permanent ownership of the chair,

a cat may occupy it.

No appeal.
