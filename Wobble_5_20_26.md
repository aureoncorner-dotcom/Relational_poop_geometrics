## 0. Framing Discipline

This document does **not** claim that late 2026 will produce collapse.

It asks a narrower question:

> If several already-visible pressure layers collide in the same window, how does the system behave?

The goal is **cascade literacy**, not prophecy.

The central claim is simple:

- The wobble is material because household room is structurally thin.
- The institutional calendar makes stress more visible.
- Material stress is the causal substrate. Everything else is observation scaffolding.

The calendar does not cause the squeeze. The squeeze comes from rent, debt, wages, energy, automation, credit conditions, geopolitical disruption, and institutional delay.

### Disposition of the symbolic overlay

Previous versions of this framework included an astrological/symbolic calendar layer (equinox hinges, Mercury retrograde, etc.) presented alongside institutional events. **That layer has been demoted to Appendix A.** It is retained only as a mnemonic device. No symbolic marker may appear in the causal stack, dashboard, or scenario logic.

## 1. Method

This framework treats late 2026 as a **sampling window**, not a forecast window.

It asks four questions:

1. Which stress channels are already active?
2. Which domains can transmit stress into other domains?
3. Which repair channels remain open?
4. What would make the framework weaker or wrong?

The framework is useful only if it stays conditional. Not *"this will happen,"* but *"if X and Y occur together, Z becomes more likely."*

```ascii
Material stress      = causal substrate
Institutional clocks = visibility windows
Dashboard            = falsification tool
Symbolic calendar    = appendix mnemonic only
```

## 2. Material Stress Base

The framework begins with measurable pressure, not symbolism. The lower household substrate is fragile because several conditions are already present.

### 2.1 Household Room Is Thin

Bottom households hold only a small fraction of total wealth — low single digits to mid-single digits depending on dataset and date. The exact number moves. The structural point remains: the lower substrate has very little cushion relative to the whole system.

### 2.2 Fixed Costs Drain First

Rent, housing, utilities, insurance, transportation, food, and healthcare absorb income before discretionary spending can circulate. When fixed costs rise, household "choice" shrinks.

### 2.3 Debt Functions as Survival Padding

Credit cards, auto loans, and other revolving debt increasingly serve as shock absorbers. That creates sensitivity to high interest rates, job loss, reduced hours, utility spikes, medical bills, and rent increases.

### 2.4 AI Pressure Is Uneven

Automation exposure is concentrated in routine cognitive and administrative work: customer service, clerical processing, back-office support, scheduling, transcription, middle coordination roles. These are not abstract jobs. They are mobility rungs. If those rungs weaken, the ladder narrows.

### 2.5 Energy Is a Regressive Pressure Channel

AI infrastructure requires real substrate: data centers, cooling, transmission, generation, grid upgrades, regional power contracts. When those costs pass into rates, they do not land evenly. Lower-income households spend a larger share of income on basic power.

**Anchor point:** EIA's February 2026 electricity update reported average revenue per kWh up **9.0% year-over-year**, with residential revenue per kWh up **7.4%**. Virginia (+26.3%) and Ohio (+21.9%) led, both data-center build-out states.

### 2.6 Surplus Routing Matters

AI productivity gains are not automatically regenerative. The key question:

> Do gains circulate into wages, lower costs, public capacity, and household room? Or do they concentrate through buybacks, moats, layoffs, and asset consolidation?

That routing choice determines whether AI stabilizes the loop or tightens it.

## 3. Exposure Bands

The "bottom 60%" is not a synchronized block. It is a layered stress field. Three bands transmit stress through different channels.

### Band 1 — Bottom 20%

| Primary exposure | Transmission signal |
|---|---|
| Rent | Eviction filings |
| Food | SNAP enrollment, food bank demand |
| Medicaid / SNAP | Coverage churn |
| Utility arrears | Shutoff notices |
| Eviction risk | Court filings |
| Transportation fragility | Used-car repos, transit gaps |

**Speed:** Fast, visible, geographically concentrated.

### Band 2 — 20th–40th Percentile

| Primary exposure | Transmission signal |
|---|---|
| Hourly wage volatility | Hours-cut data |
| Rent | Late-payment rates |
| Auto debt | Auto delinquency (especially subprime) |
| Credit card debt | Minimum-payment-only behavior |
| Childcare | Workforce-participation drops |
| Medical bills | Collections, deferred care |

**Speed:** Medium. Visible in credit data with a one- to two-quarter lag.

### Band 3 — 40th–60th Percentile

| Primary exposure | Transmission signal |
|---|---|
| Mortgage or rent squeeze | Forbearance requests |
| Healthcare premiums | ACA plan downgrades, employer plan changes |
| Student loans | Delinquency post-SAVE transition |
| Auto loans | Prime auto delinquency |
| Insurance | Coverage drops, higher deductibles |
| Job instability in routine cognitive/admin roles | Layoff announcements in mid-tier white-collar |

**Speed:** Slower, more diffuse. Often invisible until a triggering event.

> **Note:** Childcare, healthcare, and housing pressures cross all three bands. The partition is analytical, not airtight.

## 4. System Map: O-R-S-T-G-D

### Core Domains

```ascii
O = Households
    wages, debt, savings, intent, discretionary room

R = AI / Corporate Layer
    automation, surplus routing, labor decisions, mediation

S = Material Substrate
    energy, rent, grid, housing, fixed costs

G = Geopolitics
    supply chains, sanctions, conflict, oil, chips, shipping

D = Demographics
    aging, labor force, migration, care burden, worker replacement

T = Time / Delay / Return
    persistence, reporting lags, policy delay, reinjection
```

### Basic Loop

```ascii
O → R → S → T → S′ → R′ → O₂
```

Return is not repetition. The system comes back through changed structure.

### Expanded Stress Loop

```ascii
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                              │
v                                              │
[O] Households ───> [R] AI / Corporate ───> [S] Material Substrate
 wages, debt        automation, surplus      energy, rent, grid
 savings            routing, layoffs         fixed costs
        │                    │                       │
        │                    v                       v
        │              [G] Geopolitics ───────> [D] Demographics
        │               chips, oil, war         aging, labor, migration
        │                    │                       │
        └────────────────────┴──────────> [T] Delay / Return
                                                     │
                                                     v
                                            [O₂] Household Room After Return
```

### Core Logic

If O loses room, and R captures surplus, and S becomes more expensive, while G and D inject shocks, then T returns the system to O₂ with reduced capacity.

> If households are already thin, the next shock does not land on a cushion. It lands on bone.

## 5. Loop Activation Thresholds

A loop is not "active" merely because it exists. Use four levels.

| Level | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Background** | The condition exists but is not materially worsening. |
| **Active** | The condition worsens across at least two reporting periods, or shows clear deterioration in multiple indicators. |
| **Acute** | The condition worsens sharply and begins transmitting stress into another domain. |
| **Cascade** | Two or more acute loops reinforce each other within the same quarter. |

**Phase III Watch** requires cascade-level interaction, not merely the presence of three named risks.

## 7. Phase Model

| Phase | Behavior | System Signature | Risk Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I — Absorption** | System absorbs shocks, but households lose slack. | Rising debt, falling savings, higher fixed costs. | Chronic stress |
| **II — Maintenance Trap** | Institutions spend more energy preserving normal appearance. | Forbearance, metric management, delayed recognition, extensions. | Fragile stability |
| **III — Cascade Failure** | Shocks propagate faster than repair can respond. | Credit freeze, clustered defaults, layoffs, policy lag. | Break condition |

**Transition rule (revised):**

- One loop Active = stress
- Two loops Active = instability
- One loop Acute = elevated risk
- Two loops Acute simultaneously = Phase III Watch (cascade-level interaction)

The system does not fail because one thing goes wrong. It becomes vulnerable when too many repair channels close at the same time.

## 10. Scenario Tests

These are not predictions. They are conditional stress tests.

### Scenario 1 — Debt-Energy-AI Squeeze

**Trigger:** Loop A reaches Acute while Loop B remains Active and Loop E provides upward wage pressure in essential sectors.
**Behavior:** Households absorb regressive utility costs while routine cognitive labor faces continued AI displacement. Discretionary collapses.
**Failure mode:** Regressive Substrate Tax compounding.

### Scenario 2 — Credit Freeze

**Trigger:** Loop C moves Background → Acute via clustered regional bank stress.
**Behavior:** Small business lending tightens, layoffs follow, household income loss feeds delinquencies, banks tighten further.
**Failure mode:** Collateral Drag Cascade.

### Scenario 3 — External Shock Compounding

**Trigger:** Loop D (currently Acute via Hormuz) deepens or a second external shock arrives (e.g., chip-supply disruption, second shipping chokepoint).
**Behavior:** Input costs spike, margins compress, layoffs accelerate, real wages fall further, policy response lags.
**Failure mode:** External Shock Reinjection at scale.

### Scenario 4 — Demographic-Driven Service Inflation

**Trigger:** Loop E activates as care/trades labor shortages bind.
**Behavior:** Service inflation persists, Fed holds restrictive longer, debt stress rises, automation pressure increases in adjacent sectors.
**Failure mode:** Demographic Torque.

### Scenario 5 — Ugly Muddle-Through (Modal Case)

**Trigger:** Institutional deadlines are patched, FOMC decisions don't materially surprise, Q3 earnings are mixed, labor markets soften but don't crack, Hormuz disruption stabilizes at high-cost equilibrium, household stress rises without clustering into defaults.

**Behavior:**

```ascii
O: Households lose more room
        ↓
R: Firms continue selective restructuring
        ↓
S: Fixed costs remain high
        ↓
T: Stress rolls forward
        ↓
O₂: Return is worse, but not broken
```

**Failure mode:** No true cascade. Continued household erosion across all three bands. Mobility rungs continue to narrow. Phase I deepens; Phase II persists; Phase III does not arrive.

> This is the most likely outcome. The framework must be able to describe it.

## 11. Falsification Conditions

The framework weakens if these occur during Q3–Q4 2026:

- Credit card and auto delinquencies decline meaningfully across bands.
- Real wages rise for lower- and middle-income workers.
- Utility pressure stabilizes rather than accelerates.
- AI productivity gains show measurable wage or cost benefits outside shareholder returns.
- Regional bank stress remains contained; CRE losses stay localized.
- Policy response arrives before defaults cluster.
- Household savings stabilize.
- Energy buildout keeps pace with data-center demand.
- Hormuz transit normalizes; oil prices retreat.
- ACA enrollment proceeds without major household coverage loss.
- Q3 earnings broadly stable rather than guidance-cutting.

If these show up, the wobble does not vanish. But the Phase III thesis weakens substantially, and Scenario 5 (or better) becomes confirmed-modal.

The framework also weakens if **upside surprise** arrives:

- AI productivity gains genuinely circulate into mid-tier wages.
- SAVE → RAP transition produces lower monthly payments than current plans for low-income borrowers.
- Energy buildout outpaces consumption growth.
- Geopolitical de-escalation reopens Hormuz fully.

A diagnostic framework focused on downside should still be able to recognize relief when it appears.
